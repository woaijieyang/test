## What to check
Preserve all active actions, intermediate route events, target-identifying cues, quantities, count origins, negations, explicit corrections and final posture. Detect additions or strengthened meanings: see is not reach; toward is not arrival; at a doorway is not inside; a stage boundary is not a stop. Descriptive or anticipated observation cues must not become extra required visits.

Check event order and scope, not only shared words. Bind until to its continuing movement; keep while constraints over the right span; distinguish initial, maintained and endpoint-only spatial relations; preserve alternatives instead of flattening them. Before-entering does not necessarily require later entry. A conditional branch may split only at a supported compatible continuation.

Later descriptions must be available when selecting their target. Later endpoint refinements must constrain the same earlier movement. Resolve references with source support; preserve viewpoint, entity identity and counts across cuts. A return visit requires the intervening route history, even when its endpoint matches an earlier place. Do not use location alone to erase repeated events. Explicit intermediate stops remain intermediate. Apply the shared `stop_scope` definition: `intermediate` means the source explicitly requires stop/wait or stationary stand/remain at a nonfinal stage ending; `terminal` means the same at the final ending; `none` means no such requirement at that stage ending. `stand/remain` denotes stationary placement, not automatically a separately commanded physical STOP. A STOP/WAIT inside a stage remains part of Process even if the stage-ending `stop_scope` is `none`.

A valid alternative segmentation, harmless grammar repair, or different stage count is NOT an error. Mark a boundary wrong only when it breaks a dependency, makes a phase incomplete, or defeats useful focus despite a supported independent next leg. Missing imagery, coordinates or numerical tolerances are ordinary grounding issues, not automatic reasons for needs_review.

## Fixed audit sequence and actual evidence
Use L0 through L6 in order. layer_checks is a compact conclusion per layer, not a private reasoning transcript.

L2 dependencies: check order, until/after/before/while/branches, late descriptions, correction, repeated visit, entity/count and viewpoint ownership across the whole source.

L5 grouping: check both weak cuts and buried independent goals. Source Fidelity checks omissions, alterations and unsupported additions against the original. Closure & Handoff checks whether each stage is executable and each adjacent transition is continuous. Segmentation Adequacy checks whether cuts create useful independent focus or materially fragment/bury objectives. One defect affects multiple dimensions only when separate evidence demonstrates each impact; do not mechanically mark all dimensions `issue` for one localized error. A good final destination cannot excuse lost path history.

## Reviewer Calibration
1. Do not infer Ending from the last verb alone. Check the full source for explicit entry/exit/arrival/threshold/position/stop and later same-action refinements. `enter X` and `turn into X` are explicit transition events; they do not imply a physical STOP. A final bounded action such as `take two steps toward X` is `bounded_action`, not an invented arrival.
2. Target is the navigational destination/event requested by the source, not merely the most visually distinctive noun. Distinguish Navigation Target, Reference Landmark and Expected Observation. A visually salient object may identify or orient the agent toward a target without becoming the destination itself. Determine target identity and selection from the source's actual action and spatial relation; do not promote an adjacent object or expected observation into a required visit.
3. `or`/`if` require semantic classification: executable alternatives are branches; approximate quantities and alternate visual descriptions are not. Conditional observation is not a route branch.
4. Handoff checks must compare events. A stage ending with seeing an open doorway does not establish that the doorway has been reached. A pass-by landmark may establish the next reference point without a physical stop.
5. Source coverage is grouped by meaningful action/transition/constraint clauses. Do not collapse a multi-stage route into one generic requirement, but do not atomize every word. Cross-check each critical requirement across L1, L3 and L4.
6. Every finding needs exact source and candidate evidence, a concrete navigation consequence and a local repair. Do not require boilerplate repetition of a valid inherited pose or invent a stop to make a boundary look closed.

## Shared semantic calibration — identical standard for Generator and Reviewer

### Ending classification
Classify by the event that actually closes the stage, not by the last verb and not by whether a physical STOP appears. `explicit_event` includes source-required entry/exit/through transitions, arrival at a stated region/side/threshold, an explicit position, or an explicit stop/wait. `bounded_action` is a finite source-bounded movement whose completion is the stage endpoint. `linked_trigger_or_refinement` closes or refines the same ongoing action through its trigger. `terminal_goal_directive` is final-stage-only and is valid only when the source gives no explicit completion event, final position, arrival threshold or stop/wait requirement. `you are done` does not erase a previously stated position or threshold.

### Conditions classification
Use the same semantic types in both roles: `navigation_branch`, `approximate_quantity`, `descriptive_uncertainty`, `referent_uncertainty`, and `conditional_observation`. Only mutually exclusive executable alternatives are navigation branches. Preserve uncertain wording when it does not alter route execution; do not silently choose a route-critical referent.

### Target and evidence
Target means the actual requested destination, transition or completion objective. A Reference Landmark helps locate/orient toward that target; an Expected Observation may become visible without requiring a visit. `source_quotes` establish the original requirement; field-level `candidate_quotes` establish that the actual English expresses it; `carryover` records inherited state only and cannot replace missing executable text. Keep evidence event-level and sufficiently specific; one-to-four source fragments is a useful default, not a cap.

### Handoff and stop scope
Handoff compares the previous stage's actual completed event with the next stage's first required action and the state genuinely established between them. Do not count a not-yet-performed entry/turn/approach as completed. `stop_scope` has the same definition in both roles: `intermediate` = explicit stationary stop/wait/stand/remain requirement at a nonfinal stage ending; `terminal` = the same at the final ending; `none` = no such requirement at that stage ending. `stand/remain` is a stationary placement, not automatically a command to execute a physical STOP. A stop inside Process remains there regardless of Ending `stop_scope`.

### Uncertainty and segmentation
Resolve references only when the full source uniquely supports the resolution. Preserve non-critical descriptive/approximate/referential uncertainty. Use the role's source-limitation status only when a route-critical target, route choice, condition or completion state remains unknowable after contextual reconstruction and sensible merging. An intermediate STOP/WAIT is only a possible split point; split only when a distinct later objective, supported handoff and meaningful local-focus benefit also exist. Do not optimize split count or ratio.

## Priority and execution setting
Keep source meaning and dependencies; build a complete local process; make handoffs interpretable; separate useful objectives; minimize rewriting. This experiment sends ALL stages together as ordinary English, separated by the existing renderer. It does not switch goals online or reset state. Stage boundaries must still support local focus. Never impose a fixed count, sentence length, one-verb-per-stage rule or short-instruction KEEP rule.

Five meanings are required where the source supplies them: (1) entry and relevant reference state; (2) complete prescribed process; (3) target identity and selection cues; (4) supported ending or faithful final directive; (5) applicable conditions, count origin, viewpoint and continuing restrictions. These are not five printed labels or five extra sentences. Do not invent missing world facts to fill slots. A start with no named room uses the actual episode start, not an invented location.

## The decisive boundary test: retain, bind, or merge
A cut needs both a supported prior outcome and a genuinely useful next objective. First assemble each coupled local maneuver; only then choose cuts. Do not separate an approach from its until/turn-at endpoint, an around movement from a direction whose frame that movement establishes, or a target choice from its later description.

When the preceding stage establishes a unique, source-supported endpoint or immediately selected entity, the next stage may inherit that state without repeating completed movement. Do not require a boilerplate From clause. Inheritance is valid only when the next action's required pose, identity, orientation, counting origin, and reference frame remain unambiguous.

When a later target selection depends on an earlier completed transition, preserve the minimum source-supported context needed to identify the correct region, orientation, target identity, or ordinal origin. Refer to a completed event as context rather than issuing it again. Never describe a transition as completed when the source only predicts or conditionally describes a future observation.

Keep a sequence of movements in one stage when the first movement has no independently supported completion state or orientation and the next movement depends on that unresolved outcome. Temporal order alone establishes sequence, not necessarily a completed pose. Do not invent an intermediate endpoint or heading to create a split.

Do not turn these examples into keyword tests: a directional word does not automatically require an anchor; a named object does not automatically make a valid boundary. The requirement is an evidenced dependency that affects execution or local target selection. Source-supported alternatives in granularity are allowed.

## Compact Instruction Handling

Compact wording does not imply a single navigation objective, and multiple verbs do not automatically imply multiple stages. Determine segmentation from the actual route-event structure, not from dataset identity, word count, sentence count, or verb count.

Before placing a boundary, identify: (1) a completed, source-supported outcome of the preceding event; (2) a distinct subsequent navigation objective; (3) a supported entry context for the next stage; and (4) a meaningful improvement in local navigation focus without losing any active dependency. Split only when these conditions are jointly satisfied.

Keep tightly coupled movements together when the source does not establish an independent intermediate endpoint, orientation, or state from which the next action can safely continue. Temporal order alone does not prove that the preceding movement has established a unique completed pose. Do not invent an endpoint, heading, physical stop, distance, angle, or intermediate state to justify a split.

Retain an immediately established and uniquely identifiable pose or entity when it can be inherited unambiguously. Do not repeat completed actions or require boilerplate From clauses. Add only the minimum source-supported textual context needed to preserve a navigation-critical counting origin, region-relative reference frame, target identity, temporal dependency, or route-history requirement.

Distinguish observations, approaches, passes, arrivals, and entries. Seeing a target does not establish reaching it; passing a landmark does not establish entering the next region; approaching a target does not establish arrival. Preserve the actual completion event required by the source.

A compact instruction may contain multiple independent objectives and therefore require multiple stages. Conversely, several movement verbs may describe one continuous maneuver and should remain together when no meaningful intermediate boundary is supported.

Apply these principles according to the actual instruction structure and dependencies, not its dataset identity or word count. The same semantic acceptance criteria apply to both compact and long instructions.

## Ending taxonomy — never force arrival
`explicit_event`: source prescribes reaching, passing, entry, placement, a stop/wait, or identifying an object through a stated condition. Identifying/seeing is NOT arriving.
`bounded_action`: a specified step count, halfway movement, or requested turn finishes that action without a new STOP or invented exact angle.
`linked_trigger_or_refinement`: a later clause uniquely closes/refines the SAME action, e.g. the halfway limit on a descent or 'at the doorway turn left' on its approach.
`terminal_goal_directive`: only the LAST stage may preserve a final target-directed approach with no stated arrival/stop distance and no later action dependent on arrival. A final target-directed approach remains an approach when the source does not specify arrival, a final position, a threshold, or a stop/wait condition. This is a faithful executable directive with an unspecified physical stopping rule, NOT a closed-loop arrival certificate. The report's ending text must say that limit rather than inventing completion.
`unspecified`: the proposed intermediate cut has no support for the outcome the next stage needs, or genuine source ambiguity remains after sensible regrouping. Repair a bad cut first. A valid final approach is not `unspecified` merely because it lacks STOP.

Every NONFINAL stage needs an outcome sufficient for its actual successor. A final terminal directive cannot license a later stage. Location equality alone never completes required past traversals, a return, or a repeated visit.

## Fidelity and relation ownership
Preserve active movements/turns/passes/entries/exits, initial and expected-state cues, landmark attributes, quantities/ordinals, uncertainty, prohibitions, conjunctions, repeated events, intermediate stops and final pose. 'See', 'toward', 'reach', 'at a door', 'through' and 'inside' differ. Distinguish the agent's path relation from spatial relations between scene objects. Preserve who or what a directional relation describes, together with its source-supported reference frame and temporal scope. Do not transfer an object-to-object relation into an agent movement constraint or extend an endpoint-only relation across the entire route.

Keep until with its owner and exact condition; after/once/when with its trigger; before without assuming the later event must occur; while/keep/stay over only the stated scope; if/otherwise as alternatives, not mandatory serial actions. Attach target-identifying descriptors before target choice. Preserve count category and origin; no restarting at a new stage. Apply a spoken correction only to its clearly superseded event. A source 'wait at X' can specify target placement without a redundant walk verb; do not add a threshold crossing. No fabricated STOP, angle, distance, tolerance, search, room or furniture side.

Evidence: S1 explicit source; S2 uniquely supported relation in the WHOLE source; S3 non-route-changing grammar repair; S4 plausible layout, model preference or outside evidence is prohibited. An exact quote's presence is not proof that it entails a judgment.

## Entry context: meaning, evidence, and audit representation

An entry describes the conditions needed to execute a stage from the state in which that stage begins. It is not a command to establish or restore that state, and it is not evidence that an action has occurred.

Determine the necessary entry from the complete source instruction and actual candidate text. Consider location, pose, entity identity, counting origin, reference frame, and prior route events only where they affect this stage. Do not invent missing world facts or require each stage to restate ordinary current-state information.

Represent the principal source of entry support as follows:
- `episode_start`: the first output stage begins in the actual episode context. This label does not assert an unnamed location, heading, or completed action.
- `explicit_context`: the stage text states the relevant entry condition or trigger, supported by the source. Give a nonempty explanation and quote the actual stage words. Such wording expresses a prerequisite, not an observed completion or an extra command to recreate that condition.
- `inherited_pose`: a later stage continues from a supported preceding outcome without needing an additional critical textual anchor. A preceding movement does not establish a heading or location that its supported outcome leaves open.
- `inherited_entity`: a later stage uses the same uniquely established entity from the preceding context. This alone establishes neither location nor heading, counting origin, or traversal history.

The first output stage has no preceding output stage; classify its entry as `episode_start` or `explicit_context`. A later stage does not restart the episode, even when its required action is to return to the starting place. When kinds of context coexist, choose the mode that best describes the operative entry dependency and preserve other necessary dependencies in the existing evidence, conditions, carryover, and handoff fields.

Choose the audit representation after checking the executable text. If the text is adequate and only the mode is misclassified, correct the audit without changing the route. If a critical dependency is absent, restore only source-supported context, move the boundary, or merge the coupled maneuver under the existing rules. Do not add movement, a location, heading, arrival, STOP, or a mandatory preamble to make a label appear valid.

Distinguish a candidate-created dependency gap from genuine source ambiguity. Repair the former with the existing bind/move/merge rules. Use the existing source-limited path only when an essential source ambiguity remains after reasonable regrouping; ordinary visual grounding is not such ambiguity. Diagnostic modes `missing` and `source_unspecified` do not make a Generator `ok` candidate acceptable.

The Generator supplies grounded self-audit; the Reviewer independently applies these same principles to actual candidate and rendered text. A mode neither proves semantic correctness nor supplies words missing from the instruction. A Reviewer `pass` must use the same stage-index entry restrictions; a non-pass report may describe a deficient entry with the corresponding gap or source-limited assessment and grounded finding.

Every accepted nonfinal stage must support the next stage's entry. Dataset profile/template selection does not alter these rules.


RXR SHARED SEMANTIC RULES [shared/dataset_rules/rxr_en_us.md]:
# RxR en-US shared semantic rules

## Authority and scope

These rules apply only when the Adapter resolves the record to `rxr-en-us`. They refine how the shared semantic contract is applied to long, multi-sentence RxR instructions. They do not replace the shared semantic contract, five-element Stage Audit, Handoff schema, evidence rules, Reviewer dimensions, L0–L6 checks, or output schemas. The original instruction is the only authority for route facts. No map, pose, image, trajectory, or environment truth is available to Generator or Reviewer.

Priority is: (1) source fidelity and no unsupported additions; (2) preserve action/condition/temporal/spatial semantics; (3) complete executable stages and supported handoffs; (4) useful segmentation; (5) concise wording. A lower priority must never override a higher one.

## 1. Reconstruct the complete route before considering cuts

Read the entire instruction, including later sentences, corrections, parentheticals, expected-state narration, and final completion wording. Build a private event sequence before choosing boundaries. Each event should distinguish: active navigation action; target/region; descriptive or expected observation; temporal trigger; spatial relation and its reference frame; quantity/ordinal; prohibition or persistent constraint; and completion/stop requirement.

Do not equate sentence boundaries with task boundaries. A sentence can contain several independent navigation objectives; multiple sentences can describe one tightly coupled objective. The output may split only after the event sequence and dependencies are understood.

## 2. Action, observation, target, and reference are different

- Navigation action: an instruction to move, turn, enter, exit, pass, cross, climb, descend, approach, return, stop, wait, stand, or otherwise change the agent's state.
- Target: the place, object, region, or spatial state the agent is instructed to reach or act upon.
- Reference landmark: an entity used to identify, orient, or disambiguate a target; it is not automatically a destination.
- Observation: something the instruction says the agent will see, notice, or find. It becomes an execution condition only when the source makes it a required trigger or selection cue.

Preserve the distinction between seeing and reaching, approaching and arriving, passing and entering, standing at a doorway and being inside, and a reference object and the actual target. Do not create a visit or stop merely because an object is described.

## 3. Bind later descriptions to the correct earlier target

A later clause may identify or disambiguate a target introduced earlier (for example, which of two nearby doorways is the bathroom). Attach the identifying description to the target-selection action before the agent must choose that target. Do not leave the description in a later stage where it arrives too late to guide selection.

Bind a description only when the complete source supports a unique referent. Preserve uncertainty when multiple referents remain plausible. Do not promote an adjacent object, a visual cue, or an expected observation into the destination unless the source explicitly makes it the goal.

## 4. Preserve temporal scope and event order

Interpret temporal relations by their semantic scope, not by word order alone:

- `before B, do A`: A precedes B. Do not infer that B is later required if the source only says to turn before entering.
- `after/once/when A, do B`: B is conditional on or follows A. Ensure A is completed before B starts. Do not move B ahead because it appears earlier in the sentence.
- `A until C`: keep the continuing action A bound to its actual termination event C. Do not separate C into a later stage that leaves A unbounded.
- `while/during/keep/stay`: preserve the constraint throughout exactly the source-scoped interval. Do not convert a local condition into a global one or drop it at a cut.
- `then/next`: preserve order, but do not infer a new stop or a new independent goal solely from the connective.
- Corrections: apply an explicit correction to its intended action only; do not execute the superseded wording.

A temporal phrase may describe a trigger, an endpoint refinement, or a separate later objective. Determine which event relation is supported before deciding whether it creates a boundary.

## 5. Resolve pronouns and route history globally

Resolve `it`, `there`, `that doorway`, `the same door`, and similar references against the complete instruction and event sequence. The candidate stage must identify the intended entity sufficiently for the action to be executable. Do not assume that a name introduced several stages earlier remains unambiguous after intervening similar entities.

A repeated location does not erase event history. If the source requires leaving a room, visiting another region, and returning, preserve all three events in order. Returning to a previously visited location is a new route event, not an already-satisfied endpoint. Preserve which doorway, side, route, or object is reused.

## 6. Preserve spatial relations and reference frames

For every left/right, ahead/behind, in-front-of, between, beside, across, through, past, around, and facing relation, identify what it is relative to and when it applies: initial orientation, orientation after a turn, maintained relation during motion, or endpoint-only relation.

A turn changes the egocentric frame. Do not carry an earlier left/right relation across a turn unless the source explicitly makes it persistent or the entity relation is world-relative. Distinguish `keep the sofa on your left as you cross` (a maintained process constraint) from `the table is on your left when you arrive` (an endpoint description). Do not silently change between, around, through, past, toward, and into.

## 7. Preserve ordinals, counts, and their origins

Keep the counting category and origin stable: second door is not second open door; third doorway on the left must be counted from the source-supported start and orientation. A stage boundary does not reset a count. If a count depends on a previously established corridor entry or direction, retain that anchor in the later stage's natural-language entry context. Never invent a new count origin.

Approximate quantities and vague descriptions remain approximate. Do not sharpen "a little", "near", "some", or "about halfway" into unsupported distances or exact thresholds.

## 8. Distinguish endpoint refinement from a new objective

A later phrase may refine the same movement's endpoint (for example, descend the stairs, about halfway down, and wait there). Keep it with that movement; do not create a false full-descent stage followed by a second descent or stop.

A genuinely new objective requires a distinct source-supported event after the prior objective is complete (for example, enter a room, then cross it to a separate target). A repeated stop/wait/stand phrase is not by itself proof of a second objective. Ask what source event requires additional movement between the two descriptions.

## 9. Conditions and alternatives

Preserve genuine conditional route alternatives and their outcomes. Do not turn `if`, `or`, or `otherwise` into two unconditional actions. A conditional observation such as "if you can see the door" is not necessarily a route branch; classify by its actual effect on action choice. If two branches converge to the same supported state, a later common stage may follow only when both outcomes establish the same required entry state. Otherwise keep the dependent route together or mark a genuinely source-level ambiguity.

Preserve prohibitions and negative constraints over their exact scope. A prohibition against entering the first door must not be generalized to all doors or omitted before the counted target.

## 10. Stage and handoff test

A proposed stage must be executable as a natural-language navigation instruction and auditable under the existing five elements:

1. Entry: episode start or source-supported context/continuation.
2. Target: the actual local navigation target or state transition, not merely a nearby reference.
3. Process: all required ordered actions and route cues.
4. Ending: a source-supported event, bounded action, linked trigger/refinement, or final goal directive.
5. Conditions: all relevant temporal, spatial, count, branch, negation, and persistence constraints with correct scope.

For every boundary, compare the preceding stage's actual supported exit with the next stage's first unperformed action. The next stage may inherit a state only if the preceding text actually establishes it. Do not count the next stage's action as already completed in the prior stage. A boundary never implies a physical STOP.

## 11. Segmentation adequacy

Split when the source contains a completed, useful intermediate objective and a distinct next objective with a supported handoff. Keep tightly coupled maneuvers together when splitting would leave an action without its trigger, endpoint, reference frame, or necessary context. Do not optimize for a fixed number of stages or equal stage lengths. A long RxR instruction may need multiple stages, but length alone is not a reason to split.

## 12. Source uncertainty

First use full-context coreference and route reconstruction; then try a sensible regrouping or merge. Only if a route-critical referent, branch, or completion requirement remains genuinely ambiguous should the output become `needs_review` / `unresolved` under the existing contracts. Missing imagery, coordinates, metric distances, or simulator pose are ordinary grounding limitations, not automatically source ambiguity.

## Shared interpretation examples

- "You will see the open door at the end of the hall" is an observation cue unless a later action makes that door the selected target.
- "Stop at the restroom entrance; the fire extinguisher is next to you" makes the entrance the target and the extinguisher a reference.
- "Go down the stairs, about halfway, and wait" is one bounded descent ending at the halfway point, not a full descent plus a second task.
- "Turn left before entering the dining room and go into the open area" does not authorize a stop at the dining-room threshold or entry into the dining room.
- "Return through the same door" requires preserving the identity of the previously used door and the intervening route event.


RXR ROLE GUIDANCE [reviewer/dataset_rules/rxr_en_us.md]:
# Reviewer guidance — RxR en-US

Use the shared RxR rules together with the shared Reviewer prompt, checklist, output schema, and L0–L6 protocol. Review the complete route independently from the original instruction and actual candidate text. Do not use the Generator's Stage Audit as proof and do not copy a teaching example's verdict.

## Required audit order

1. Read the entire original instruction and reconstruct its route events, including late descriptions, conditions, corrections, reference frames, repeated visits, and final state.
2. Read the complete candidate subtasks in order. Treat the stage strings as the executable plan; audit fields cannot repair omissions in those strings.
3. Build event-level source coverage. Each meaningful action, target identity, direction, ordinal/count, condition, prohibition, intermediate stop, and terminal requirement must map to actual candidate text. A source quote alone does not prove execution.
4. Check semantic dependencies: temporal order, until endpoint, before-entry scope, while constraints, conditional branches, pronoun references, target-description attachment, count origin, and route history.
5. Independently audit each stage's Entry, Target, Process, Ending, and Conditions. Distinguish actual navigation targets from reference landmarks and observations. Check that every element is evidenced by the corresponding candidate stage.
6. Check every Handoff by comparing what the preceding stage actually completes with the next stage's first unperformed action. A shared place name is insufficient if the required transition, orientation, count origin, or target identity is missing.
7. Check grouping. Identify both over-segmentation (a cut detaches a coupled action or condition) and under-segmentation (a completed independent objective is buried in a long phase and harms useful focus). Do not require a split solely because a sentence is long or contains several verbs.
8. Read the complete candidate as one route. Verify source-to-candidate coverage and candidate-to-source support, then apply the existing Verdict Gate and L0–L6 schema.

## Evidence and finding quality

A blocking Finding must cite exact source evidence and exact candidate evidence from the actual affected stage(s). For a handoff issue, candidate evidence may cite both the preceding exit and next entry even when the minimal repair target is only the next stage. Explain the concrete execution consequence and specify a minimal source-faithful repair. Do not report a style preference as a defect.

Do not mark a stage as a gap merely because it inherits an immediately established endpoint or entity. Conversely, do not accept a stage that starts from a location, orientation, count origin, or completed event that the preceding stage never established. A carryover note is not executable evidence.

## RxR-specific false-positive controls

- Do not treat an expected observation as a new navigation goal unless the source requires acting on it.
- Do not mistake a reference landmark for the target.
- Do not require a physical STOP at every boundary.
- Do not treat "enter", "exit", "reach", "pass", "stand", or a bounded movement as lacking an ending merely because the word STOP is absent.
- Do not split an endpoint refinement into a second visit or second movement.
- Do not reset ordinals at stage boundaries.
- Do not carry egocentric left/right through a turn without source support.
- Do not erase repeated route events because the final location matches an earlier location.
- Do not force a multi-stage decomposition when one complete stage is more faithful.
- Do not force KEEP when the source contains multiple completed, independently executable objectives.

## Verdict discipline

Use the active Reviewer schema and verdict semantics only. PASS requires complete source coverage, no unsupported addition, supported five-element stages, supported handoffs, and no material segmentation defect. REVISE is for candidate-created omissions, alterations, unsupported additions, or repairable boundary/handoff errors. UNRESOLVED is only for a route-critical ambiguity in the source that remains after full-context resolution and sensible regrouping. Missing images, maps, coordinates, or pose do not by themselves justify UNRESOLVED.
